# Belgium
Email marketing in Belgium is governed by Book XII of the Code of Economic Law (Article XII.13), the Royal Decree of 4 April 2003 which sets out the exceptions, and the EU GDPR together with the Belgian Data Protection Act of 30 July 2018.

## Content required
Advertising must be clearly identifiable as such, and the person or organisation behind it must be identifiable. A sender must not use a third party's identity or falsify or conceal information showing the message's origin. Every message must also state the recipient's right to object and provide an electronic means of opting out. Separate business-disclosure rules may require a geographic address to be made readily available, but Article XII.13 does not specifically require a postal address in every marketing email.

## Consent
You can only send marketing emails to those who have provided prior, free, specific and informed consent to receive marketing emails from you. The burden of proving consent lies with the sender.

This can be either by having a double opt-in or by checking an explicit checkbox while subscribing.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
The Royal Decree of 4 April 2003 allows two exceptions to the opt-in rule: a soft opt-in for existing customers (where the details were obtained during a sale and you are marketing your own similar products or services, with an opt-out offered), and emails to impersonal corporate addresses of legal persons (such as info@company.be).

As Belgium follows GDPR, organisations must appoint a data protection officer in certain circumstances. These include public authorities and organisations whose core activities involve large-scale regular and systematic monitoring or large-scale processing of special-category or criminal-offence data. The officer advises and monitors compliance, while the organisation remains responsible for meeting its data-protection obligations.

## Penalties
Infringements of the Code of Economic Law carry criminal fines, decided on a case-by-case basis.
The maximum fine available under the GDPR is up to 20 million EUR, or 4% annual global turnover – whichever is higher.
