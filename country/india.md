# India
India still has no dedicated anti-spam law covering email marketing. However, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act) was enacted in August 2023, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules 2025 were notified in November 2025. As email addresses are personal data, email marketing will be regulated under the DPDP Act as consent-based processing.

The Act is being implemented in phases: the Data Protection Board was established in November 2025, Consent Manager registration begins one year after the 13 November 2025 notification, and the substantive obligations (notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting and penalties) commence 18 months after that notification, in May 2027.

## Content required
None mandated for the email itself at present. Once the DPDP Act's obligations take effect, any request for consent must be accompanied by an itemised notice setting out what data is collected, the purpose, how to withdraw consent and how to complain to the Data Protection Board.

## Consent
Not yet enforced. From May 2027, consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given with a clear affirmative action and limited to the specified purpose. Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it – effectively an opt-out requirement for consent-based email marketing.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
Under the Information Technology Act 2000, Section 67 restricts publishing 'obscene' content in electronic form. Compensation may also be due under the same act if data is held insecurely and stolen – though that provision is repealed when the substantive DPDP Act provisions commence in May 2027.

The DPDP Act requires verifiable parental consent before processing the personal data of children under 18.

## Penalties
The DPDP Act's substantive penalties are not enforceable until the relevant provisions commence in May 2027. The Data Protection Board will then be able to impose penalties of up to 250 crore INR (2.5 billion INR) for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, up to 200 crore INR for failing to notify a breach or for breaching children's data obligations, up to 150 crore INR for breaching Significant Data Fiduciary obligations, and up to 50 crore INR for other violations.
