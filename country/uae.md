# United Arab Emirates
The UAE has no single general anti-spam statute governing every marketing email. Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2023 on Trading by Modern Technological Means gives consumers dealing with in-scope merchants the choice whether to receive advertising and marketing by email, phone or social media. The federal Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) also gives individuals the right to object to, or stop, processing for direct-marketing purposes. Its executive regulations and Cabinet decision setting administrative penalties had not been issued as of July 2026.

The Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority's (TDRA) Unsolicited Electronic Communications Regulatory Policy is sector-specific. It applies directly to licensed telecommunications providers, and its detailed marketing-message provisions focus on mobile text messages. It should not be presented as a general email-marketing law binding every business in the UAE.

## Required content
There is no single statutory content template for every ordinary marketing email. The requirements depend on the consumer, privacy, sector and free-zone rules that apply. Clear sender identification and a free, effective opt-out are prudent, but the TDRA's mobile-message requirements should not be described as universally applicable email-content rules.

## Consent
There is no single universal consent rule for all marketing email. Consumers covered by the Modern Technology-Based Trade Law must be allowed to choose whether to receive advertising and marketing, while the PDPL gives data subjects a right to object to or stop direct-marketing processing. The TDRA policy separately requires licensed providers to obtain prior consent for their own marketing communications and to regulate marketing text messages sent over their networks. Free-zone and sector-specific regimes may impose different requirements.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
The TDRA policy's two-year record-retention provision is directed at licensed providers and mobile text-message marketing; it is not a general retention rule for every email marketer. The Dubai International Financial Centre and Abu Dhabi Global Market have separate data-protection regimes that may apply instead of the federal PDPL.

Separate telemarketing rules (Cabinet Decisions 56 and 57 of 2024, which replaced the 2023 rules and extended them to social media messaging) require licences, restrict calling hours and impose a Do Not Call Registry – these target calls and messaging rather than email, but enforcement under them is active.

## Penalties
There is no single general email-specific maximum penalty. TDRA enforcement is directed at licensed telecommunications providers, while consumer-protection, cybercrime, sector-specific and free-zone penalties may apply depending on the facts. The federal PDPL's administrative penalties are to be set by Cabinet decision.
