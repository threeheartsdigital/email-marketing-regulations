# United Arab Emirates
Spam in the UAE is regulated by the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority (TDRA, formerly the TRA) under its Unsolicited Electronic Communications Regulatory Policy. The UAE also has a federal Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021), which gives individuals an unconditional right to stop their data being processed for direct marketing – however, its executive regulations have still not been issued as of 2026, so the PDPL remains effectively unenforced for now.

## Required content
Marketing messages must identify the sender and include a free, effective opt-out. The TDRA's policy drafts these duties around text messages and licensed telecoms operators, but they set the standard email senders are expected to follow in practice.

## Consent
The TDRA's policy defines spam as marketing electronic communications sent without the recipient's consent, making the UAE an opt-in jurisdiction in practice. There are no B2B or legitimate-interest carve-outs, and pre-checked boxes or bundled consent aren't compliant.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
Telecoms licensees must keep evidence of consents and opt-outs for two years after the last message, and produce it to the TDRA on request.

Separate telemarketing rules (Cabinet Decisions 56 and 57 of 2024, which replaced the 2023 rules and extended them to social media messaging) require licences, restrict calling hours and impose a Do Not Call Registry – these target calls and messaging rather than email, but enforcement under them is active.

## Penalties
The TDRA can impose penalties of up to 10 million AED for violating the unsolicited communications framework. The PDPL's penalties are still to be set by Cabinet decision alongside its executive regulations.
