# United Arab Emirates
The UAE has no single general anti-spam statute governing every marketing email. Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2023 on Trading by Modern Technological Means gives consumers dealing with in-scope merchants the choice whether to receive advertising and marketing by email, phone or social media. The federal Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) also gives individuals the right to object to, or stop, processing for direct-marketing purposes. Its executive regulations and Cabinet decision setting administrative penalties had not been issued as of July 2026.

The Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority's (TDRA) Unsolicited Electronic Communications Regulatory Policy is sector-specific: it applies directly to licensed telecommunications providers, and its detailed marketing-message provisions focus on mobile text messages. It isn't a general email-marketing law binding every business – but it defines spam as marketing sent without the recipient's consent, and it sets the standard regulators expect for electronic marketing in the UAE.

## Required content
There is no single statutory content template for every ordinary marketing email – the requirements depend on the consumer, privacy, sector and free-zone rules that apply. In practice, you should clearly identify the sender and include a free, effective opt-out in every message, which is the standard the TDRA's policy sets for marketing messages on UAE networks.

## Consent
There is no single universal consent rule for all marketing email, but every applicable regime points the same way: treat the UAE as opt-in. Consumers covered by the Modern Technology-Based Trade Law must be allowed to choose whether to receive advertising and marketing, the PDPL gives data subjects the right to object to or stop direct-marketing processing, and the TDRA policy defines spam as marketing sent without consent (with no B2B or legitimate-interest carve-outs – pre-checked boxes and bundled consent don't count). Free-zone and sector-specific regimes may impose different requirements.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
The TDRA policy's two-year record-retention provision is directed at licensed providers and mobile text-message marketing; it is not a general retention rule for every email marketer. The Dubai International Financial Centre and Abu Dhabi Global Market have separate data-protection regimes that may apply instead of the federal PDPL.

Separate telemarketing rules (Cabinet Decisions 56 and 57 of 2024, which replaced the 2023 rules and extended them to social media messaging) require licences, restrict calling hours and impose a Do Not Call Registry – these target calls and messaging rather than email, but enforcement under them is active.

## Penalties
Penalties depend on the applicable regime. Under the telecommunications framework, fines of up to 10 million AED per violation can be imposed – though this enforcement is directed at licensed providers. Consumer-protection, cybercrime, sector-specific and free-zone penalties may also apply depending on the facts. The federal PDPL's administrative penalties are still to be set by Cabinet decision.
