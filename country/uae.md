# United Arab Emirates
Spam in the UAE is regulated by the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority (TDRA, formerly the TRA) under its Unsolicited Electronic Communications Regulatory Policy. The UAE also has a federal Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021), which gives individuals an absolute right to object to direct marketing – however, its executive regulations have still not been issued as of 2026, so the PDPL remains effectively unenforced for now.

## Required content
Marketing messages must identify the sender and include a free, effective opt-out. Senders must also provide recipients specific circumstances under which their personal data is collected and used, if requested.

## Consent
The TDRA's policy defines spam as marketing electronic communications sent without the recipient's consent, making the UAE an opt-in jurisdiction in practice. There are no B2B or legitimate-interest carve-outs, and pre-checked boxes or bundled consent aren't compliant.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
Senders must keep records of how personal data is collected, processed and used.

Separate telemarketing rules (Cabinet Resolutions 56 and 57 of 2023, extended to social media messaging in 2024) require licences, restrict calling hours and impose a Do Not Call Registry – these target calls and messaging rather than email, but enforcement under them is active.

## Penalties
The TDRA can impose penalties of up to 10 million AED for violating the unsolicited communications framework. The PDPL's penalties are still to be set by Cabinet decision alongside its executive regulations.
