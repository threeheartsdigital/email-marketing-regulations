# Brazil
A legislation called Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados Pessoais (LGPD) took effect in September 2020, with penalties becoming enforceable from August 2021. The LGPD is made up of 65 articles. Articles 17-22 deal with the rights of data subjects, those whose data is collected and/or processed, so mainly individuals or natural persons. It has 10 legal bases for the processing of personal data, four more than the GDPR. The national data protection authority (ANPD) is now actively enforcing the law, having issued its first fines in 2023.

## Content required
All emails should include name and contact information. Emails should also include a clear unsubscribe/opt-out option. There's no clear mention of the time period necessary to process an unsubscribe.

## Consent
The sending of unsolicited communications must be done based on one of the two legal grounds for processing: consent, and controller's legitimate interest.

In February 2024, the ANPD published its Guide on Legitimate Interest, confirming that legitimate interest can be a legal basis for direct marketing – subject to a documented balancing test. You must tell data subjects at collection that their data will be used for direct marketing, explain it in your privacy policy, and honour the right to object. Consent remains the safer basis, and purchased lists are treated as presumptively non-compliant.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
The Email Marketing Self-Regulation Code (CAPEM) is a project undertaken by ISPs. The code is not legally binding and the ISPs only agree voluntarily to participate. It contains basic rules to protect internet users and asks marketers to include an opt-out link in every communication sent.

Soft opt-in is also allowed where an existing commercial or social interest can be demonstrated (effectively legitimate interest).

## Penalties
According to LGPD, non-compliance will result in the following sanctions:
- Warning, with an indication of the period for adopting corrective measures.
- Simple fine of up to 2% of the revenue in Brazil of a private legal entity, group or conglomerate. The revenue is based on that for the prior financial year (excluding taxes) up to a total maximum of 50 million BRL per infraction.
- Daily fine, subject to the total maximum referred above.
