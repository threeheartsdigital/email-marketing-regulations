# Brazil
Brazil's Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados Pessoais (LGPD) took effect in September 2020, with penalties becoming enforceable from August 2021. The LGPD is made up of 65 articles. Articles 17-22 deal with the rights of data subjects, those whose data is collected and/or processed, so mainly individuals or natural persons. It has 10 legal bases for the processing of personal data, four more than the GDPR. The national data protection authority (ANPD) is now actively enforcing the law, having issued its first fines in 2023.

## Content required
No Brazilian statute prescribes the content of marketing emails, but the LGPD's transparency principles mean emails should identify the sender, and a clear unsubscribe/opt-out option is expected. There's no defined time period for processing an unsubscribe.

## Consent
The sending of unsolicited communications must be done based on one of the two legal grounds for processing: consent, and controller's legitimate interest.

In February 2024, the ANPD published its Guide on Legitimate Interest, confirming that legitimate interest can be a legal basis for direct marketing – subject to a documented balancing test. You must tell data subjects at collection that their data will be used for direct marketing, explain it in your privacy policy, and honour the right to object. Consent remains the safer basis, and purchased lists are hard to justify under either basis, since the people on them were never told at collection that you'd be marketing to them.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
Brazil once had an industry self-regulation code for email marketing (CAPEM), created in 2010 by advertising and internet industry bodies. It was never legally binding and now appears dormant – its websites no longer resolve.

There is no statutory soft opt-in. In practice, marketing to existing customers is handled through the legitimate interest basis, with the ANPD's balancing test applied and documented.

## Penalties
According to LGPD, non-compliance will result in the following sanctions:
- Warning, with an indication of the period for adopting corrective measures.
- Simple fine of up to 2% of the revenue in Brazil of a private legal entity, group or conglomerate. The revenue is based on that for the prior financial year (excluding taxes) up to a total maximum of 50 million BRL per infraction.
- Daily fine, subject to the total maximum referred above.
