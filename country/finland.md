# Finland
Finland, being part of the European Union, follows the GDPR that governs email marketing and data processing. They also have the Act on Electronic Communications Services (917/2014), which implements the E-Privacy Directive – its direct marketing rules are in Sections 200 and 202-204. Both are used to regulate email marketing.

## Content required
Marketing emails must be clearly recognisable as marketing, must not disguise or conceal the sender's identity, and must include a valid address the recipient can use to opt out. Unlike some countries, the law doesn't require a postal address in the email itself.

## Consent
You can send marketing emails to those who have provided prior consent to receive marketing emails from you (an active opt-in – pre-ticked boxes don't count).

Email is also allowed if the recipient has not specifically opted out and if you obtain the customer's contact details in the context of sale of a product or service, provided you gave them the chance to opt out when their details were collected. You cannot sell the data, however you can use the contact information for direct marketing of your own similar products. This is essentially soft opt-in using implied consent.

Businesses (legal persons) may be emailed without prior consent, unless they have prohibited it. This is aimed at organisational addresses – emailing a named employee's work address generally still needs consent, unless the offer clearly relates to their role.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
As Finland follows the GDPR, some companies (for example, those processing personal data on a large scale) must appoint a data protection officer, who is in charge of maintaining and enforcing data protection standards.

## Penalties
The maximum fine available under the GDPR is up to 20 million EUR, or 4% annual global turnover – whichever is higher. The Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman enforces the direct marketing rules and has fined companies for email marketing without consent.
