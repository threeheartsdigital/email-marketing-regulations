# Finland
Finland, being part of the European Union, follows the GDPR that governs email marketing and data processing. They also have the Act on Electronic Communications Services (917/2014), which implements the E-Privacy Directive – its direct marketing rules are in Sections 200-202. Both are used to regulate email marketing.

## Content required
All emails sent for marketing purposes should clearly identify the sender, including the sender's name and mailing address. Messages must be clearly recognisable as marketing and include a valid, free and easy way to opt out. The law treats these as compulsory information for your marketing emails.

## Consent
You can send marketing emails to those who have provided explicit consent to receive marketing emails from you.

Email is also allowed if the recipient has not specifically opted out and if you obtain the customer's contact details in the context of sale of a product or service. You cannot sell the data, however you can use the contact information for direct marketing of your own similar products. This is essentially soft opt-in using implied consent.

Businesses (legal persons) may be emailed without prior consent, unless they have prohibited it.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
As Finland follows the GDPR, some companies (for example, those processing personal data on a large scale) must appoint a data protection officer, who is in charge of maintaining and enforcing data protection standards.

## Penalties
The maximum fine available under the GDPR is up to 20 million EUR, or 4% annual global turnover – whichever is higher. The Office of the Data Protection Ombudsman enforces the direct marketing rules and has fined companies for email marketing without consent.
