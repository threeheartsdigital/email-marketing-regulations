# Hong Kong
The Unsolicited Electronic Messages Ordinance (UEMO) regulates the sending of unsolicited commercial electronic messages in Hong Kong. It came fully into force on 22 December 2007 and is enforced by the Office of the Communications Authority (OFCA). The Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (PDPO) adds a further layer: before using someone's personal data for direct marketing, you must notify them and obtain their consent or no objection. A wider PDPO reform (including direct fining powers for the privacy regulator) is under consultation but had not been enacted as of mid-2026.

## Content required
All emails sent for marketing purposes must include accurate sender identification – the sender's name plus functional contact details (a telephone number, and a contact email address for email messages), valid for at least 30 days after sending. Subject lines must not be misleading. Every message needs a functional unsubscribe facility, and unsubscribe requests must be honoured within 10 working days.

## Consent
The UEMO is an opt-out regime: prior consent is not required to send commercial electronic messages with a Hong Kong link, provided the recipient hasn't opted out. However, where you are using personal data for direct marketing, the PDPO requires notification and consent first – effectively an opt-in layer on top.

## Penalties
UEMO penalties are tiered. For ordinary breaches of the sender information and unsubscribe rules, OFCA first issues an enforcement notice – breaching that notice carries a fine of up to 100,000 HKD. Using techniques such as address harvesting or dictionary attacks carries a fine of up to 1 million HKD and up to 5 years' imprisonment, and fraud offences involving multiple messages can attract an unlimited fine and up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Under the PDPO, using personal data in direct marketing without the required consent carries a fine of up to 500,000 HKD and 3 years' imprisonment, rising to 1 million HKD and 5 years where data is provided to others for gain.
