# Denmark
Denmark has two laws that govern email marketing and other forms of electronic marketing. These are the Danish Marketing Practices Act (Section 10 of which contains the spam prohibition) and EU GDPR. Both laws combined cover all aspects of electronic marketing in Denmark. The spam prohibition is enforced by the Consumer Ombudsman.

## Content required
All emails sent for marketing purposes should clearly identify the sender, including the sender's name and mailing address. The law treats these as compulsory information for your marketing emails.

## Consent
You can only send marketing emails to those who have provided prior, voluntary, informed and specific consent to receive marketing emails from you. This applies to consumers and businesses alike.

This can be either by having a double opt-in or by checking an explicit checkbox while subscribing with your privacy policy link mentioned there.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
There is an existing-customer exception (soft opt-in): if you obtained the email address during a sale, you may market your own similar products, provided an opt-out was offered when the details were collected and in every message since.

As Denmark follows GDPR, some companies (for example, those processing personal data on a large scale) must appoint a data protection officer, who is in charge of maintaining and enforcing data protection standards.

## Penalties
Spam fines follow a tiered model in force since 2022: 20,000 DKK for up to 30 messages (also the minimum fine – a single spam email has attracted a 20,000 DKK fine), 30,000 DKK for 31–50 messages, 50,000 DKK for 51–100 messages, and 50,000 DKK plus 200 DKK per message beyond 100. Repeat offences add 20%.

The maximum fine available under the GDPR is up to 20 million EUR, or 4% annual global turnover – whichever is higher. The Danish Data Protection Agency is the governing body for data protection breaches.
