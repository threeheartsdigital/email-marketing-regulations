# Iceland
Iceland is part of the EEA rather than the EU, and applies the GDPR through its Data Protection Act No. 90/2018. Email marketing itself is governed by Article 94 of the Electronic Communications Act No. 70/2022, which requires prior opt-in consent for direct marketing by email. The Electronic Communications Office (Fjarskiptastofa) enforces the marketing rules, while the Data Protection Authority (Persónuvernd) oversees data protection.

## Content required
Every marketing email must clearly identify the sender, including the sender's name and mailing address.

## Consent
You can only send marketing emails to those who have provided prior, informed consent to receive marketing emails from you.

This can be either by having a double opt-in or by checking an explicit checkbox while subscribing with your privacy policy link mentioned there.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
There is a soft opt-in for existing customers: you may market your own products or services to them, provided a free opt-out was offered at sign-up and is included in every message. General corporate email addresses (such as info@) may be used for B2B marketing.

As Iceland applies GDPR through the EEA, some organisations (public authorities, or those doing large-scale monitoring or large-scale processing of sensitive data) must appoint a data protection officer.

## Penalties
For breaches of the email marketing rules, Fjarskiptastofa can impose administrative fines of up to 4% of a company's total turnover for the preceding financial year (10,000 to 10 million ISK for individuals). Under the Data Protection Act, fines range from 100,000 ISK up to 2.4 billion ISK or 4% of annual global turnover for serious violations.
