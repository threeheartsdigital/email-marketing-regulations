# Singapore
Email marketing in Singapore is regulated by two main laws; the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) and the Spam Control Act 2007. The PDPA governs the collection, use and disclosure of personal data by organizations. The Spam Control Act aims to control unsolicited commercial communications sent electronically.

## Required content
Singapore has strict guidance for email content. All marketing email campaigns are required to be truthful (not deceitful) and comply with the principles of fair competition. Furthermore, content must align with Singapore's family values. The Spam Control Act requires senders to:

- Include accurate sender identification and contact information (email, postal address or telephone number)
- Provide a link or email address for the recipient to unsubscribe, valid for at least 30 days after sending
- Honour unsubscribe requests within 10 business days
- Include "&lt;ADV&gt;" in the email subject line

## Consent
Under the PDPA, subscribers must provide consent before they can receive emails. Regulations encourage email marketers to require subscribers to submit a signature or provide acknowledgment that they agree to receive emails. Consent can be express or deemed in some circumstances.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
The PDPA requires email marketers to have policies in place to comply with these regulations and inform other members of staff. There must also be a method to receive complaints about the handling of personal data.

Singapore's Do Not Call Registry covers telephone numbers only (calls, SMS and fax) – it does not apply to email addresses.

## Penalties
Since October 2022, violations of the PDPA can result in a financial penalty of up to 10% of an organisation's annual turnover in Singapore (where turnover exceeds 10 million SGD) or 1 million SGD, whichever is higher. Under the Spam Control Act, businesses or individuals can face statutory damages of 25 SGD per unsolicited email, up to a total of 1 million SGD.
