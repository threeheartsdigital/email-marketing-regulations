# Singapore
Email marketing in Singapore is regulated by two main laws: the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) and the Spam Control Act 2007. The PDPA governs the collection, use and disclosure of personal data by organisations. The Spam Control Act aims to control unsolicited commercial communications sent electronically.

## Required content
Singapore's advertising industry code (the Singapore Code of Advertising Practice, administered by the Advertising Standards Authority of Singapore) expects marketing to be truthful, respect fair competition and align with Singapore's family values – though this is a voluntary code rather than statute. The Spam Control Act requires senders of unsolicited commercial email sent in bulk to:

- Include accurate, non-misleading sender and header information, with a functional email address or telephone number
- Provide a link or email address for the recipient to unsubscribe, valid for at least 30 days after sending
- Honour unsubscribe requests within 10 business days
- Include "&lt;ADV&gt;" in the email subject line

## Consent
Under the PDPA, organisations generally need consent before using an individual's personal data for direct marketing, subject to statutory exceptions. Consent does not need to take a particular form, but organisations should keep records that demonstrate it. The PDPA generally excludes business contact information, such as a business email address used for business purposes, from its data-protection rules. Deemed consent by notification cannot be used for direct marketing, although other forms of deemed consent may apply where their statutory tests are met.

The Spam Control Act itself does not require prior consent. Instead, its labelling, sender-information and unsubscribe rules apply when unsolicited commercial email is sent in bulk.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
The PDPA requires email marketers to have policies in place to comply with these regulations and inform other members of staff. There must also be a method to receive complaints about the handling of personal data.

Singapore's Do Not Call Registry covers telephone numbers only (calls, SMS and fax) – it does not apply to email addresses.

## Penalties
Since October 2022, violations of the PDPA can result in a financial penalty of up to 10% of an organisation's annual turnover in Singapore (where turnover exceeds 10 million SGD) or 1 million SGD, whichever is higher. Under the Spam Control Act, businesses or individuals can face statutory damages of 25 SGD per unsolicited email, up to a total of 1 million SGD.
