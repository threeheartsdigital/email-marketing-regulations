# Japan
In Japan, unsolicited email communications are regulated by the Act on Specified Commercial Transactions (ASCT) and the Act on the Regulation of Transmission of Specified Electronic Mail (Anti-Spam Act). The Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI) also applies to the handling of subscriber data – an amendment passed in July 2026 will give the regulator the power to impose administrative fines for certain categories of violation, such as improper acquisition or unlawful third-party provision of personal data, expected to take effect by 2028.

## Required content
Advertising emails must identify the sender or organisation responsible for sending them. They must state that the recipient can opt out and provide the email address or URL used to do so, as well as the sender's address and contact details for enquiries or complaints. Official guidance allows some of this information to appear on a directly accessible linked page. Sender information must not be falsified.

## Consent
Both acts require email marketers to obtain prior consent (opt-in) from recipients before sending marketing emails, with limited exceptions such as an ongoing business relationship. Under the ASCT, proof of consent must be kept for three years following the last email. Under the Anti-Spam Act, consent records must be kept while sending continues and for at least one month after the last message.

## Notable exceptions or requirements
There is no clear time period for which opt-out or unsubscribe requests must be honoured. However, the legislation does stipulate that no other emails can be sent once the recipient has opted out. Sending to randomly generated addresses is prohibited.

## Penalties
For individuals, violations carry a fine of up to 1 million JPY or 1 year of imprisonment with labour. Corporations can be fined up to 30 million JPY under the Anti-Spam Act where an officer or employee commits the violation.
